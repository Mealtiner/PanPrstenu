Myth one: to come to a LARP you have to fence. Myth two: you need a five-thousand-crown costume. Myth three: you have to be a Tolkien enthusiast who's read the Silmarillion three times.

None of these are true. The Battle for Middle-earth runs every year on people who **don't hold a weapon** — and without them the event couldn't happen. Here's a starting point if any of these roles speak to you.

## Camp helpers

[Helpers](/en/role/pomocnici/) are the load-bearing wall of the organisation. They help with registration, setting up the camp, carrying things, first aid, cleanup. It's not dirty work — helpers have their own space in the story (they're often part of one of the armies' narratives) and they see the event from an angle a regular participant never catches.

If you don't know where to go but want to be useful — this is the easiest way in.

## Vendors

The medieval market is as much a part of the event as the battle. Jewellers, leatherworkers, smiths, drink brewers, costume sellers — [vendors](/en/role/stankari/) build the atmosphere participants remember. If you have a craft or wares that fit Middle-earth, there's a spot for you here.

## Photographers and cameramen

This year will have several [official photographers and cameramen](/en/role/fotografove-a-kameramani/). If you enjoy your camera and want to try reportage with depth — this is your role. You move across the whole event, have access where regular participants don't, and after the event you help put together the next gallery.

The videos on the home page came from exactly this kind of work. Next year they could be yours.

## Non-combat hobbit escort

This role is for people who want to be in Middle-earth but don't need to fight. [Hobbits](/en/role/hobiti/) travel, run their own micro-quests, play in the evening, cook in the morning. For parents, partners of participants and generally people who want the atmosphere without contact combat, it's the most accessible entry.

For a parent's-eye view we have a separate [For parents](/en/pro-rodice/) page with information about safety, escorting children and how the event works for a family.

## Organisers

If you feel like going deeper and running a piece of the action yourself, the [organising team](/en/role/organizatori/) is open. We're looking for people who can lead a group, have patience with logistics, sort things both by phone and in person. It's not volunteer work for everyone, but if this side of organisation appeals to you, get in touch.

## Supporting participants: money, not time

There's one more way to support the event — and it doesn't require you to travel. [Supporting participants](/en/podpor-ucastniky/) is a programme where you can pay the registration fee for someone who otherwise couldn't afford the event. Specifically, young people from children's homes, students, Ukrainian participants.

If you have a few extra crowns and want this meadow to welcome someone who couldn't otherwise come, this is the fastest way.

## Time pressure

A small note at the end. **Registration for the main game as well as for non-combat roles opens on 18 May**, and the first price tier ends on **30 June 2026**. After that date the registration fee goes up.
