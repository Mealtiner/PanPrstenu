The Battle for Middle-earth stretches back over three decades. In that time, so many people have crossed the meadows at Pulkovský mlýn (and elsewhere in past years) that if you lined them all up in a single battle row, they'd reach from one forest to the other. We counted **33 editions**, opened the older chronicles, and here's a short retrospective.

## Attendance over the years

We started small — a few dozen players, one meadow, two campfires. Today the event is many times bigger and a new generation arrives every year.

The numbers are rounded — the archives from the 1990s are incomplete, and the count of costumed guests has always been a "give or take" figure.

## Nine armies, two sides

From the start it has been played as a duel of **Free Peoples versus Forces of Darkness**, only the number of armies has grown over time. Today there are nine:

- **Free Peoples:** [Gondor](/en/frakce/gondor/), [Rohan](/en/frakce/rohan/), [Elves](/en/frakce/elfove/), [Dwarves](/en/frakce/trpaslici/)
- **Forces of Darkness:** [Orcs](/en/frakce/skreti/), [Uruk-hai](/en/frakce/skuruti/), [Harad](/en/frakce/harad/), [Umbar](/en/frakce/umbar/)
- **Mercenaries:** [Hill-men of the Highlands](/en/frakce/vrchovina/) — they fight for whoever pays

The balance of forces between the sides differs from year to year. On average a few more people ride for the Free Peoples, but **2018** historically belongs to the Orcs and Uruk-hai — that was the year when Sauron, for the first time in the modern era, really pushed the defenders back to the Wedge.

## A few numbers that matter for 2026

- **~500 participants** are expected this year. Most of the veterans already know which army they want to join.
- **4 days** of event (Thursday → Sunday), but the main battle is only on Saturday.
- **9 armies** = 9 different camps, 9 different aesthetics and 9 different camp games.
- **Children from 12** in the main game, younger ones in their own [children's game](/en/detska-hra/).

You can browse the full [statistics](/en/minule-rocniky/statistiky/) by year — there's also a [year-vs-year comparison](/en/minule-rocniky/statistiky/srovnani/) showing how the armies have shifted over time.

## Why we're writing this

Because tradition doesn't mean "the same as last year". The Battle for Middle-earth shifts a little every edition — costumes get better, game mechanics get refined, the story opens new directions. But what doesn't fade is 33 years of accumulated experience and 33 years of people coming back.
